# Evidence Classification

Evidence classification prevents different kinds of information from appearing more certain than they are.

## The five classes

| Class | Meaning | Example | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Observed | A source reported a condition or event | A vehicle appears in a recent public feed | Coverage may be partial and does not prove the complete experience |
| Scheduled | A published plan describes intended service or activity | A trip appears in a timetable | Does not prove the activity occurred |
| Estimated | A transparent calculation approximates a condition | A grouped population share within a walking screen | Depends on assumptions, source coverage, and method |
| Modeled | A versioned method explores, simulates, or forecasts a result | A disruption scenario | Is not an observed outcome or official decision |
| Contextual | Information adds meaning to the question | Population, schools, health facilities, or employment centres | Does not by itself prove service quality or impact |

## Required metadata

Each published measure should retain:

- Evidence class
- Source and source owner
- Source timestamp or reference date
- Retrieval or calculation time
- Geographic and temporal coverage
- Quality state
- Method and version, where applicable
- Missing fields or suppression
- Plain-language limitation

## Combination rule

Evidence classes may appear together, but they must not be silently blended into one score. A combined measure requires:

1. A documented civic purpose
2. A transparent method
3. Defined weights or decision rules
4. Sensitivity testing
5. A clear interpretation boundary
6. Versioning and review

## Interface rule

Classification should be visible where it changes interpretation. Use direct labels such as:

- Observed in a recent public feed
- Published schedule
- Estimated using grouped population and a walking assumption
- Modeled scenario
- Community context, not proof of service performance

Avoid relying on a distant methodology page to correct a stronger impression created by the primary interface.
